## Context

An EdTech platform serving Indonesian working professionals needed a Tableau 101 course that took absolute beginners from install to a presentable dashboard. The existing market either over indexed on tool clicks (without business framing) or jumped straight into advanced features. The brief was a course that respects a working professional's time and lands them on something portfolio worthy.

## Problem

- Mixed learner background. Some had Excel fluency, others were starting from zero. The first three modules had to scaffold confidence without boring the experienced ones.
- Tutorial content easily becomes "click here, then click here" without teaching the underlying decisions. Learners finish, can't reproduce on a different dataset, and lose momentum.
- A single dataset had to carry the whole arc, otherwise every module felt disconnected.

## Approach

1. Sequenced the curriculum in five blocks: Introduction & Preparation (install, joins, data cleaning), Visualization Basics (filters, parameters, sorting), Geospatial Maps, Calculations (table calcs, LOD), and Interactive Dashboards + Trend Lines & Forecasting. Each block ends with a checkpoint exercise rather than a quiz.
2. Built a single case study, VoraStore, a fictional multinational retailer operating across four US regions (Central, East, South, West) with three product categories (Furniture, Office Supplies, Technology). Every module reuses VoraStore so concepts compound instead of resetting.
3. Anchored the case study in a CEO brief: total sales, profit, return orders, product performance, profit ratio by state, and a profitability crisis to investigate. The learner plays a BI Analyst, not a tool user.
4. Wrote slides and speaker notes in Bahasa Indonesia with key technical terms preserved in English, matching how analysts actually talk on the job.
5. Designed each module to land on a tangible artifact, ending with a full interactive dashboard the learner can show in a portfolio or internal review.

## Outcome

The course shipped as a complete syllabus with slides, study case brief, and a dashboard the learner builds across the modules. Instead of a tool walkthrough, learners finish with a defensible BI artifact they can talk through in an interview. The VoraStore case study is reusable across cohorts and gives the platform a consistent reference example for future intermediate courses.
