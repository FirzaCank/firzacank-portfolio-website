## Context

An EdTech platform for Indonesian working professionals needed a Tableau 101 course taking absolute beginners from install to a presentable dashboard. The market either over-indexed on tool clicks (no business framing) or jumped straight to advanced features. The brief: a course that respects a professional's time and lands them on something portfolio-worthy.

## Problem

- Mixed backgrounds — some Excel-fluent, others from zero. The first three modules had to build confidence without boring the experienced.
- Tutorials easily devolve into "click here, then here" without teaching the underlying decisions, so learners can't reproduce on a new dataset and lose momentum.
- A single dataset had to carry the whole arc, or every module felt disconnected.

## Approach

1. Sequenced five blocks: Introduction & Preparation (install, joins, data cleaning), Visualization Basics (filters, parameters, sorting), Geospatial Maps, Calculations (table calcs, LOD), and Interactive Dashboards + Trend Lines & Forecasting — each block ending with a checkpoint exercise, not a quiz.
2. Built one case study, VoraStore — a fictional multinational retailer across four US regions (Central, East, South, West) with three categories (Furniture, Office Supplies, Technology). Every module reuses it so concepts compound instead of resetting.
3. Anchored it in a CEO brief: total sales, profit, return orders, product performance, profit ratio by state, and a profitability crisis to investigate. The learner plays a BI Analyst, not a tool user.
4. Wrote slides and speaker notes in Bahasa Indonesia with key technical terms kept in English, matching how analysts actually talk.
5. Designed each module to land on a tangible artifact, ending with a full interactive dashboard for a portfolio or internal review.

## Outcome

The course shipped as a complete syllabus — slides, case brief, and a dashboard the learner builds across modules. Instead of a tool walkthrough, learners finish with a defensible BI artifact they can talk through in an interview. VoraStore is reusable across cohorts and gives the platform a consistent reference example for future intermediate courses.
