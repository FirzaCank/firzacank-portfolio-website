## Context

An e-commerce affiliate operation earning commission through video content that drives marketplace transactions. Commission revenue had fallen four months straight — a ~34% cumulative drop. Leadership needed a clear diagnosis and recovery plan before the trend turned structural.

## Problem

Three questions before scoping any fix:

- Where in the funnel (plays → impression → click → order) is volume actually leaking?
- Is the decline a rate problem (CVR/CTR falling) or a volume problem (fewer plays/impressions)?
- Are there internal levers (commission mix, SKU sourcing, retention) to recover revenue without external brand subsidies?

## Data & preparation

The funnel (plays → impression → click → order), commission, and cohort data came from the platform's internal database exports. Before any analysis, I cleaned and reconciled the raw extracts:

- Deduplicated transaction records and handled null/missing values across the funnel tables.
- Reconciled funnel-stage counts whose sources didn't align (plays/impressions vs click/order systems), so each stage compared on a consistent base.
- Normalized dates into consistent monthly cohorts (January–April).
- Standardized commission rates and Rupiah amounts (direct vs indirect, standard ~2% vs extra ~4% tiers) into a single clean schema.

## Approach

1. Built a full-funnel comparison (January baseline vs April) across plays, impressions, clicks, and orders, isolating the bottleneck at each stage.
2. Decomposed commission into direct vs indirect channels — indirect (clicking one SKU but buying another) carried 97% of revenue and 99% of the decline.
3. Analyzed CTR and CVR independently: CTR was flat, confirming the problem was purchase intent (CVR had dropped meaningfully), not attention.
4. Ran cohort retention across Jan–Mar cohorts, surfacing a significant collapse in month-1 retention — the most alarming structural signal.
5. Found the commission-mix lever: a minority of orders earned the higher-tier rate, so shifting the mix toward better-tier SKUs could recover meaningful monthly revenue at existing volume.
6. Packaged findings into a 10-slide executive deck with a two-track recovery plan (quick wins 0–60 days, structural levers 60–180 days) and three leadership asks.

## Outcome

The deck became the working document for Q2 planning. Three decisions followed:

- **Immediate:** Product approved a 3-day SKU refresh cycle (down from 7) to combat ad fatigue, and began testing personalized SKU recommendations to recover CVR.
- **Short-term:** BD prioritized targeted-affiliate negotiations with priority brands to unlock higher commission tiers.
- **Structural:** Growth scoped a retention program for the Jan–Mar cohorts before the active base shrank further.

Projected recovery, if all three tracks execute: 50–70% of the monthly gap within two quarters.
