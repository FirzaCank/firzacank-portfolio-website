## Context

An AI assistant for gaming creators that auto-turns long gameplay streams into highlight clips. It sits between gameplay capture and social distribution (TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Reels), so every onboarded creator either becomes a recurring clip source or churns silently. Growth had plateaued, and the founding team wanted to know whether the bottleneck was acquisition, activation, or content output per creator.

## Problem

Internal dashboards reported headline numbers but missed the cohort view. Three questions the team couldn't answer with confidence:

- Which onboarding step quietly bleeds the most users before they generate a clip?
- Do power creators behave differently from the median in week one of the AI clip generator?
- Where does the funnel break for users who connect gameplay capture but never publish a clip?

Without that, every roadmap discussion turned into opinion.

## Approach

I scoped the analysis around the publish event (an AI-generated clip going live on a connected social account) as the activation proxy, then worked backward.

1. Pulled a 90 day event extract via SQL from the platform's internal event store, then joined it with subscription state and the AI clip generation log.
2. Cleaned the raw events before modeling: deduplicated and sessionized the event stream, filtered out internal test and bot accounts, reconciled mismatched join keys across the event ↔ subscription ↔ clip-log tables, and handled null and invalid timestamps (including timezone normalization).
3. Built a Python notebook for cohort retention, funnel decay, and AI feature usage per persona (auto-clip, manual edit, batch export).
4. Stress tested three persona splits (casual streamer, semi-pro, agency / multi-channel) against publish rate, AI clip generation frequency, and session cadence.
5. Wrapped the findings in a Tableau workbook plus a 12 slide pitch deck for the leadership read-out.

## Outcome

Three things changed after the read-out:

- A specific onboarding step was identified as the largest drop-off point and rewritten the next sprint.
- Power creators showed a distinct day-one feature pattern that became a new in-product activation checklist.
- The team agreed on a single north-star metric (creators publishing an AI-generated clip in week one) and retired four vanity charts.

The deck became the working document for the next two quarterly planning rounds.
