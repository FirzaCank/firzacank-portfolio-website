## Context

A financial-analyst brief on a publicly listed Indonesian e-commerce platform. The thesis: margin expansion, not revenue growth, was the next leg of the story. The deck had to defend that with a credible bottom-up forecast, a DCF, and a regional-peer comparable read — in a format that holds up in an investment committee.

## Problem

The narrative existed but the numbers were thin. Three gaps to close:

- A bottom-up revenue forecast breaking out 3P vs 1P contribution.
- A DCF anchored on plausible FCF and capex assumptions for 2024–2029.
- A comparable set placing the platform next to the right regional peers and explaining the valuation gap.

## Approach

1. Sourced the inputs from the client alongside public filings and market data — the company's published financial statements, exchange/market figures, and regional peer disclosures — then built a five year P&L and FCF model in Excel with explicit drivers for take rate, gross margin, and capex intensity.
2. Ran a DCF with WACC sensitivity and a terminal-growth band, generating a fair-value heat map.
3. Pulled a SEA-marketplace peer set and benchmarked EV multiples, revenue growth, and EBITDA margin.
4. Used Python to render supporting charts (FCF/capex stacks, sensitivity heat map, valuation-gap bars).
5. Assembled an executive-summary deck plus a longer due-diligence appendix.

## Outcome

The deck ran in two investor conversations and a partner briefing. The valuation-gap chart and the FCF sensitivity heat map anchored the discussion. The model was handed off as a working file so the client could refresh the case on each quarterly print.
